Planning around the study

Connect the property analysis to the complete tax picture.

A cost-segregation study can identify property components with different recovery periods. The planning value depends on when the property was placed in service, the quality and cost of the study, the expected holding period, participation, income, and other taxpayer facts.

Outside-Provider Study

An outside provider prepares the cost-segregation study and identifies property components and recovery periods. Golden Years reviews how that study may fit the client’s tax plan; it does not prepare the study.

Depreciation Timing

Reclassifying eligible components may move depreciation deductions into earlier years. Golden Years considers basis, placed-in-service timing, projected income, holding period, future sale considerations, and study cost before treating the timing as useful.

Loss Usability

Accelerating depreciation does not by itself determine whether a resulting loss can offset other income. Activity classification, material participation, passive-loss and at-risk rules, records, and the taxpayer’s facts all affect the analysis.

Common cost-segregation questions

Understand the opportunity before making the move.

These general answers describe the planning framework. Individual treatment requires a review of the property, activity, records, and complete tax position.

When does a cost segregation study actually make sense?

That depends on the property type, basis, placed-in-service timing, expected holding period, tax position, study quality, and study cost. A review should happen before treating projected deductions as a benefit.

Can cost segregation offset W-2 or business income?

Cost segregation can change the timing of depreciation deductions. Whether resulting losses are usable against W-2 or business income depends on the activity, material participation, passive-activity and at-risk rules, records, and the taxpayer’s facts.

How does Real Estate Professional Status affect the analysis?

Qualifying for Real Estate Professional Status and materially participating in a rental activity are separate parts of the analysis. Neither status alone resolves passive-loss treatment; activity-level participation, at-risk limits, records, and the taxpayer’s complete facts still matter.

Coordinated planning situations

Review cost segregation in the context where it will be used.

The same study can produce different planning outcomes for different activities, portfolios, income years, and owners.

Short-Term Rentals

For STR activity, average stay, services provided, material participation, activity classification, and contemporaneous records can affect how a resulting loss is analyzed.

Multiple Properties

Acquisition dates, placed-in-service timing, participation by activity, holding plans, and property-level records should be reviewed across the portfolio rather than assumed to produce one result.

High-Income Years & Business Owners

A high-income year may make timing especially important. Business owners can coordinate real estate with entity structure, retirement planning, cash flow, deduction timing, and exit planning without assuming the resulting losses are currently usable.
